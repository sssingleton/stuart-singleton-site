I.Why the sun's height

Low sun crosses more air: about 2× at 30°, 5.6× at 10°, almost 40× at the horizon. Air scatters blue far more than red, so the longer the path, the warmer the beam. That is golden hour.

After sunset the beam is gone and only the sky is left, which is why blue hour is blue.

II.Why altitude

Up high there is less air over you and most haze sits in the lowest mile or so. Same sun, cleaner and cooler light: at 10° the model reads about 4100K in Nashville and 4700K in Denver.

Mountains still block the sun early; the model can't see terrain.

III.Film, not auto

Daylight film was fixed near 5500K, so sunsets came out gold. Setting the white balance to the exact Kelvin of the light cleans that away. The slider moves part of the way from 5500K toward neutral, so you choose how much of the mood stays.

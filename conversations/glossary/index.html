Glossary

Conversations55 termsUpdated November 17, 2026

The terms that come up across Conversations, defined in plain English. In each post, hover over or tap a dotted term to see its definition. Music business terms describe how things work in the US unless they say otherwise.

Organizations

A2IM
The American Association of Independent Music, a US trade group for independent labels. Official site
American Music Fairness Act
A bill in Congress (H.R. 861 and S. 326) that would make US AM/FM radio pay royalties to recording artists and master owners. It has not passed. Official site
ASCAP
The American Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers, a US PRO founded in 1914. Open to any songwriter. Official site
BMI
Broadcast Music, Inc., a US PRO. Open to any songwriter. Official site
CineStill
A company that sells Kodak motion picture film with the antihalation backing removed, for still cameras. Official site
Content ID
YouTube's system that matches uploads against registered audio and video, so rights holders can earn from, track, or block them. Official site
GMR
Global Music Rights, an invitation-only US PRO founded in 2013. Official site
Goldman Sachs Music in the Air
A yearly music industry report from Goldman Sachs that forecasts streaming, publishing, live, and superfan revenue. Official site
Heliograph
A lab on stuartsingleton.com that uses a photo's time and place to model sunlight and suggest a white balance starting point. Official site
HFA (Harry Fox Agency)
A US mechanical licensing agency founded in 1927. It licenses and collects mechanical royalties for publishers, mostly outside The MLC's blanket license, such as physical copies and direct deals. Official site
Loud & Clear
Spotify's yearly report on what it pays the music industry and how many artists reach each earnings level. Official site
Luminate
A music and entertainment data company. Its data powers the Billboard charts, and it publishes yearly reports on listening and fans. Official site
LyricFind
A lyrics licensing company that supplies lyrics to search engines and apps and pays publishers. Official site
MRI (Music Reports)
Music Reports, Inc., a US company that licenses music and pays mechanical royalties to publishers on behalf of services, outside The MLC's blanket license. Official site
Musixmatch
A lyrics licensing company that supplies lyrics to streaming apps and pays publishers. Official site
Patreon
A membership platform where fans pay creators monthly or per post. Official site
PRO (performing rights organization)
Collects performance royalties for songwriters and publishers when songs are played on radio, TV, in venues, and on streaming. The US PROs are ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, and GMR.
SESAC
A US PRO that writers join by invitation. It also owns the Harry Fox Agency. Official site
Songtrust
A publishing administrator that registers songs with societies and pay sources worldwide for a commission. Official site
SoundExchange
The US nonprofit that collects royalties for recordings played on internet and satellite radio, such as Pandora and SiriusXM, and splits them 50% to the master owner, 45% to the featured artist, and 5% to session players and singers. Official site
The MLC
The Mechanical Licensing Collective, a nonprofit designated under US law to collect and pay streaming and download mechanical royalties under the blanket license. Free for self-administered songwriters. Official site
Universal Audio
A company that makes audio interfaces and plug-ins modeled on classic analog hardware. Official site
US Copyright Office
The federal office that registers copyrights. Registration is needed before suing for infringement of a US work. Official site
USISRC
The US ISRC agency, run by the RIAA. Sells registrant codes so artists and labels can assign their own ISRCs. Official site

Codes

IPI number
Interested Party Information number. The ID a PRO gives each writer and publisher so their registrations can be matched worldwide.
ISRC
International Standard Recording Code. A 12-character code that identifies one recording. Distributors give them for free, or you can buy your own from USISRC. Official site
ISWC
International Standard Musical Work Code. Identifies a song (the composition), and is assigned when the song is registered with a society such as a PRO. Official site
UPC
Universal Product Code. The barcode number that identifies a release, such as a single or album. Official site

Rights and royalties

Composition
The song itself: melody, chords, and lyrics. Its copyright belongs to the songwriters or their publishers, and its income is called publishing.
Master
One specific recording of a song. Its copyright is separate from the song itself and usually belongs to whoever paid for the recording.
Mechanical royalties
Royalties paid to songwriters and publishers when a song is reproduced: on a stream, a download, or a physical copy. In the US, 13.1¢ per song per copy for physical and downloads in 2026.
Neighboring rights
Royalties for recordings played publicly outside the US, including on radio, paid to performers and master owners through local societies.
Performance royalties
Royalties paid when a song is performed publicly: radio, TV, venues, streaming. Collected by PROs for the composition.
Points
A producer's or artist's percentage of a master's royalties. Not ownership of the recording.
Publishing
The money a composition earns, usually split into the writer's share and the publisher's share.
Publishing administrator
A company that registers your songs and collects your publishing royalties for a commission, usually around 10 to 20%, while you keep ownership.
Reciprocal agreement
A deal between collection societies in different countries to collect for each other's members, so a song played abroad gets paid back home.
Split sheet
A document listing every writer on a song, their percentage, publisher, PRO, and IPI number. Every registration copies from it.
Streamshare
Spotify's method of paying: a rights holder's share of all eligible streams in a market sets its share of that month's royalty pool. Official site
Sync license
Permission to pair music with picture, such as film, TV, ads, or video. Not compulsory: the rights holders set the price or say no.
Work made for hire
Under US law, a work where the person or company paying is treated as the author. It changes who owns the copyright and blocks termination rights. Official site

Fans

1,000 true fans
Kevin Kelly's 2008 idea that a creator can make a living from 1,000 fans who each bring in $100 of profit a year. Official site
CPI
The Consumer Price Index, the US government's main measure of inflation, published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Official site
Superfan
A listener who engages and spends far more than average. Definitions vary by study.

Sound and light

Bayer filter
A grid of red, green, and blue filters over a camera sensor, invented at Kodak in 1976. Each pixel measures one color and the other two are estimated.
Bit depth
How precisely each digital audio sample is measured. Each bit adds about 6 dB of range.
Demosaicing
Estimating the two missing colors at each pixel of a Bayer sensor from its neighbors.
Halation
A glow around bright highlights in film, caused by light passing through the film, reflecting off the back of the base, and re-exposing the emulsion.
Hybrid mixing
Mixing mostly in a DAW while sending selected tracks or the mix through analog hardware for color.
In the box
Mixing entirely inside a computer, with a DAW and plug-ins. "Out of the box" means using outboard hardware or a console.
LUT
A lookup table that maps input colors to output colors. Most film presets are LUTs.
Metamerism
When two different spectra of light produce the same color values, to a sensor or to the eye.
Rayleigh scattering
Scattering of light by air molecules, much stronger for blue than red. It makes the sky blue and low sun warm.
Sampling theorem
Sampling a signal at more than twice its highest frequency allows it to be rebuilt exactly. The basis of digital audio.
Saturation
The gradual, rounded distortion tape and tubes add as they are pushed, which adds harmonics and softens peaks.

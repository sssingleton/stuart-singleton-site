Where does your Spotify money actually go?

Music businessNo. 04By Stuart SingletonUpdated November 17, 20267 min read

Stuart asks

If someone pays for Spotify and never plays my music, does any of their money still end up with me, or does it all go to the biggest artists?

The short answer

Spotify doesn't pay a set rate per stream. It puts roughly two-thirds of what it earns from music into a pool for each country, then splits that pool by each rights holder's share of all streams. Your subscription doesn't follow what you play. If you never play an artist, none of your money goes to them, but it doesn't go to just one top artist either. It's spread across everyone in proportion to their streams, so most of it goes to the artists with the most streams. And since April 2024, a track has to reach 1,000 streams in 12 months before it earns any recording royalties.

How does Spotify decide what to pay?

Each month, in each market, Spotify adds up subscription and ad money, sets aside the royalty pool, and divides it by streamshare: the share of all eligible streams that a rights holder's music got. Spotify's own example: if a track gets 1% of all streams, it gets 1% of that month's pool.

So a "per-stream rate" is an average worked out afterward, not a price. That's why the commonly quoted figure, about $0.003 to $0.005 per stream, moves around.

What are royalty calculators actually telling you?

An average. Since Spotify doesn't have a per-stream price, a royalty calculator can't look one up. Most take a per-stream figure from industry estimates, usually somewhere around $0.003 to $0.005, and multiply it by your streams. Some let you pick a country or adjust for premium versus free listeners.

Your real number depends on where your listeners are, how many pay for premium, what the total pool was that month, and what your distributor or label keeps. Treat a calculator as a rough range for the recording side, before your deal. Most leave out the publishing side entirely.

Does my subscription go to the artists I listen to?

Not directly. Under streamshare, everyone's money goes into one pool per market and gets divided by total streams. A subscriber who never plays you still adds to the pool you're paid from, but your cut depends on your share of everyone's listening, not theirs.

The flip side: artists with the most streams get the most from every subscriber, including people who never play them. The money isn't handed to one top artist. It's spread by share, and the biggest shares belong to the biggest catalogs.

You're paid from everyone's subscriptions, by your share of everyone's listening.

What is the 1,000-stream rule?

Since April 1, 2024, a track needs at least 1,000 streams in the previous 12 months, plus a minimum number of unique listeners that Spotify doesn't publish, to be included in the recorded royalty pool. Tracks below that earn nothing for those streams, and their share goes back into the pool for eligible tracks.

Spotify says tracks under 1,000 yearly streams averaged about $0.03 a month and made up 0.5% of streams. The rule applies to recording royalties only. Publishing royalties are calculated as before.

How much does Spotify pay out overall?

Spotify says it paid the music industry more than $11 billion in 2025, nearly $70 billion since launch, and that roughly half of royalties were generated by independent artists and labels. About 13,800 artists generated at least $100,000 from Spotify in 2025, and the 100,000th artist generated about $7,300.

Why does my statement show less than that?

Because Spotify doesn't pay anyone directly as an artist. It pays the companies that deliver and administer the rights. If you own your master, you are the rights holder, but your recording money still reaches you through your distributor, which takes a fee or a share under your deal. If a label owns or controls the master, it gets paid and then pays you under your contract.

The song side is paid separately. A stream pays both copyrights in a song: the master through your distributor or label, and the composition through your publisher or admin, your PRO, and The MLC in the US.

Frequently asked questions

Does Spotify pay a fixed rate per stream?

No. Spotify pools roughly two-thirds of its music revenue in each market and splits it by each rights holder's share of streams. Per-stream figures, often $0.003 to $0.005, are averages worked out afterward.

Does my Spotify subscription go to the artists I listen to?

Not directly. All subscriptions in a market go into one pool that is divided by total streams, so artists with the most streams get the largest share of every subscriber's money.

What is the Spotify 1,000-stream rule?

Since April 1, 2024, a track needs at least 1,000 streams in the previous 12 months, plus an unpublished minimum of unique listeners, to earn recorded royalties.

How much did Spotify pay out in 2025?

More than $11 billion to the music industry, according to Spotify's Loud & Clear 2026 report.
